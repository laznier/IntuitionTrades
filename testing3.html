dbHJ Risk Assessment Tool

This tool uses a double Heston stochastic volatility jump‐diffusion (dbHJ) model—with jump‐risk mitigation calibrated via live options pricing—to estimate the risk of a given stock over a future time horizon.

Detailed Explanation

This tool uses a Monte Carlo simulation based on a double Heston stochastic volatility jump‐diffusion (dbHJ) model. It is designed for jump‐risk mitigation by modeling both the stock price dynamics and two independent stochastic volatility processes combined with jumps.

  • Double Heston Component: Two variance processes (v1 and v2) are simulated. Each follows a Heston dynamic:
    d\(v_i\) = κi(θi − vi)dt + σi√vi dWv_i
  • Jump-Diffusion: On top of the diffusive price dynamics, a Poisson jump process is added to capture sudden large movements. The jump intensity (λ), mean jump size (μJ), and jump volatility (σJ) govern the jump risk.
  • Calibration: Live options data (via our internal liveoptions.js endpoint) provides an implied volatility estimate. This is used to set the initial variances (v1 and v2) and calibrate our model parameters.
  • Risk Metrics: The simulation produces a distribution of final stock prices. Risk is quantified by:
    • VaR (Value at Risk): The 5th percentile of the simulated price distribution.
    • CVaR (Conditional Value at Risk): The average loss in the worst 5% of scenarios.
  • Interactive Features: An adjustable slider sets a target price threshold. A red dashed vertical line on the histogram (updateable in real time) indicates this threshold, and the tool shows the probability that the stock ends above the target price.
