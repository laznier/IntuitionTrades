SPY Market Regime & Weight Distribution

Detected SPY Regime: ${simulationReport.regime}

We automatically analyze SPY's short vs. long SMA plus the daily closing slope over the last ~4 months to classify the broader market as Bull, Bear, or Neutral. This influences how we weight each factor:

Bear Market

In a Bear regime, the system applies smaller weights to Monte Carlo and Technical because short-term technical patterns can be highly volatile and less reliable when the overall market is trending downward. Meanwhile, heavier weights go to Insider and Fundamental factors, as strong fundamentals and insider buying can better signal resilience in a bear phase. A smaller scaling factor (e.g., 0.25) is used to keep the final aggregated result more conservative, recognizing the downward pressure of a bear trend. 

Bear Weights (example):
Monte Carlo = 5%
Technical   = 5%
Sentiment   = 10%
Insider     = 30%
Fundamental = 30%
Congress    = 20%
Scaling     = 0.25

Because these percentages sum to 1, each factor’s raw bullish score is first averaged according to the distribution above. Then, we shift that average around 50 and apply only a 25% scale (meaning the final aggregator is depressed toward neutrality).

Neutral Market

Monte Carlo, Technical, Sentiment, Insider, Fundamentals, and Congress each receive moderate allocations, and the scaling factor is around 0.50. This scenario recognizes that the market lacks a strong directional bias, so each factor contributes roughly equally:

Monte Carlo Simulation using Geometric Brownian Motion (GBM)

Bullish Probability: ${simulationReport.mcBull.toFixed(2)}%

Drift (μ): ${simulationReport.mcStats.mean.toFixed(4)}

Volatility (σ): ${simulationReport.mcStats.stdDev.toFixed(4)}

Sₜ = S₀ × exp[(μ – 0.5σ²) × T + σ × √T × Z]

Explanation:

  • S₀: The current stock price at the last available historical date.
  • μ: Annualized drift, computed as mean of daily log returns × 252 (trading days/year).
  • σ: Annualized volatility, computed as stdev of daily log returns × √252.
  • T: Time in years from the last historical date to the future date.
  • Z: Random draw from a standard normal distribution (Z ~ N(0,1)).

The simulation runs 10,000 iterations, generating a distribution of final prices. The Bullish Probability is the % of runs where the final price exceeds the current price. You can also choose a different target price via the slider and see the probability of exceeding it.

Combined Technical Indicators

Technical Bullish Score: ${simulationReport.techBull.toFixed(2)}%

This score is the arithmetic mean of four indicators (RSI, Bollinger Bands, MACD, and WMA), each mapped into a 0–100 bullish range. A score near 100 suggests strong technical buy signals, while near 0 suggests strong sell signals.

Indicator Breakdown

  • RSI (Relative Strength Index):
    Uses a logistic-based transform around RSI=50 and accounts for daily change in RSI: 
    rel = 50 - RSI
Base_RSI = 100 / (1 + exp(-0.05 × rel))
dRSI = RSI(previous) - RSI(current)
Score_RSI = Base_RSI × (1 + 0.01 × dRSI)
  • Bollinger Bands:
    We compute %B and apply: 
    %B = (Price - Lower Band) / (Upper Band - Lower Band)
Base_BB = 100 / (1 + exp(3 × (%B - 0.5)))
d%B = %B(previous) - %B(current)
Score_BB = Base_BB × (1 + 0.1 × d%B)
  • MACD (Regression-Based Scoring):
    1) Position Score (logistic function on latest MACD)
    2) Gap Trend Score (polynomial regression to predict next MACD-signal gap)
    3) Momentum Score (polynomial regression on MACD histogram)

    The final MACD score is the average of these three sub-scores.

  • WMA (Weighted Moving Average): 
    x = (Price / WMA) - 1
Base_WMA = 100 / (1 + exp(-10 × x))
dx = x(previous) - x(current)
Score_WMA = Base_WMA × (1 + 0.5 × dx)

Technical Bullish Score = (RSI + Bollinger + MACD + WMA) / 4

Sentiment Analysis (Logistic Transformation)

Sentiment Bullish Score: ${simulationReport.sentBull.toFixed(2)}%

We gather news articles, convert each to a score from –1 to +1, then transform that into 0–100. We fit a simple linear trend to these hourly averages. The slope is passed through a logistic function (so extremely positive slopes approach 100, negative slopes approach 0), and the final sentiment score is an average of the slope-based score and the overall mean sentiment. 

Score = 0.5 × (100 / [1 + e^(-k × slope)]) + 0.5 × (average sentiment)

k is a scaling constant. This ensures that extremely steep trends yield near 0 or 100, while moderate slopes land in a mid-range.

Insider Trading Analysis

Insider Bullish Score: ${simulationReport.insiderBull.toFixed(2)}%

Insider Score = ((Total Buys - Total Sells) / (Total Buys + Total Sells)) × 50 + 50

The system filters insider transactions by date (within the historical period) and share price (within ±50% of the current price). Acquisitions add to "buys" and dispositions add to "sells." A net positive means more buying than selling, increasing the bullish score above 50.

Congress Trading Analysis

Congress Bullish Score: ${simulationReport.congressBull.toFixed(2)}%

Congress Score = ((Total Purchase Amount - Total Sale Amount) / (Total Purchase Amount + Total Sale Amount)) × 50 + 50

We collect publicly reported congressional trades over the chosen period, separate them into purchases and sales by dollar amount, and compute a net ratio. If total purchase amounts exceed sales, the Congress Bullish Score is above 50; if sales dominate, it goes below 50.

Fundamental Analysis (Expanded Explanation)

Fundamental Score: ${simulationReport.fundamentalBull.toFixed(2)}%

This tool calculates five fundamental category scores for a given ticker:

  • Profitability
  • Valuation
  • Growth
  • Dividend & Return
  • Financial Health

Each category includes multiple metrics from the Alpha Vantage OVERVIEW API. We normalize each metric on a 0–100 scale. Some metrics are directly scored (where higher is better), while others are inverted (where lower is better). Next, we average the metrics within each category for its score, and finally average across all five categories to get the aggregated fundamental score.

Normalization Method

We use simple linear normalization from min to max:

ratio = (value - min) / (max - min)
score = clamp(ratio × 100, 0, 100)

If a value exceeds max, it caps at 100. Below min goes to 0. Some metrics (like P/E) are inverted: invertedScore = 100 - norm(value). The table below shows common ranges used:

MetricTypical BoundsInterpretation
Profit Margin 0 to 25% (0.25) Margin > 25% is very high (score near 100)
P/E (Price-to-Earnings) 5 to 40 Lower P/E is better → invert the normalization
Beta 0 to 2 Beta=1 is neutral (~50); Beta=0 is stable (~100)

Profitability

Considers Net Profit Margin, Operating Margin, Return on Assets, and Return on Equity. We assume typical upper ranges, then average the four normalized values for a single Profitability Score.

Valuation

Looks at P/E Ratio, PEG Ratio, Price-to-Sales, and Price-to-Book. Since lower numbers typically suggest cheaper valuations, we do 100 - norm(value) and average them into the Valuation Score.

Growth

Includes Quarterly Revenue Growth (YOY), Quarterly Earnings Growth (YOY), plus the difference between current price and Analyst Target Price (as a ratio). We normalize each and average them for the Growth Score.

Dividend & Return

Combines Dividend Yield, Dividend per Share, and Earnings per Share. We assume typical max bounds for each, normalize, then average them for the Dividend & Return Score.

Financial Health

Looks at EBITDA Margin, EV/EBITDA (inverted), and Beta (also inverted around 1). The average of these is the Financial Health Score.

Final Aggregation

After computing each category score, we simply take their average:

Aggregated Fundamental Score
= (Profitability + Valuation + Growth + Dividend & Return + Financial Health) / 5

A higher score implies stronger fundamentals relative to these generic thresholds, but always consider industry context and do your own research.
